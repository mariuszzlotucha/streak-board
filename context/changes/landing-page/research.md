## Research Question

Taken from `frame.md` and the owner's decisions recorded there and in `change.md` (2026-10-08): a stranger arriving from a public link to `streakboard.app` must reach a page they can trust, which promises "a shared goal and visibility" (English), links a privacy policy, and leads to sign-up or sign-in; a signed-in visitor goes from `/` to `/dashboard`; the Google submissions happen after the deploy and gate `/10x-archive`. What must `/10x-plan` know?

1. What the public surface serves today, and which parts still carry the starter template or hide the brand.
2. Which contracts constrain rewriting `/` and redirecting signed-in visitors.
3. Which UI building blocks and in-app wording the copy can rely on, and which promises the code cannot keep.
4. What a truthful privacy policy has to state (data, recipients, retention, cookies, deletion), and the legal frame around it.
5. What Google requires: the Safe Browsing review, OAuth Branding while the app stays in Testing, and whether strangers can sign in with Google.
6. Which tests, release checks and documents change.

## Summary

- **The brand is missing where passwords are typed, and starter leftovers are still public.** `/` carries product copy since `35b08ea`, but it keeps the starter's look (`bg-cosmic`, `Welcome.astro:5`), the favicon comes from the `init` commit, and `https://streakboard.app/template.png` serves the "10x-astro-starter" banner (HTTP 200, 1,266,671 bytes, 2026-10-08). Outside `<title>` and `<meta>`, the production HTML of `/auth/signin`, `/auth/signup` and `/auth/confirm-email` contains the string `StreakBoard` 0 times; the first two have a password field. Google's social-engineering guide names the test "whether a user viewing the page in isolation understands which site they are on". That these pages caused the flag is an inference; Google gave no sample URL.
- **The flag needs a review of the site, not a fix of one page.** The Transparency Report says "Some pages on this site are unsafe" (status 3), and Search Console shows one issue, "Deceptive pages", with no sample URLs, so no page can be singled out; Google's guide says to request a review in that case once all deceptive content is gone, and that "a review can take several days". Other Google pages give other durations (a day to weeks), and a site that "repeatedly" switches between compliant and noncompliant is blocked from reviews for 30 days.
- **Plain HTTP is served.** `http://streakboard.app/` and `http://streakboard.app/auth/signin` answer 200 without a redirect to HTTPS; the HTTPS responses carry no `Strict-Transport-Security`. The Supabase session cookies use the library defaults (`path`, `sameSite: "lax"`, `httpOnly: false`, `maxAge` 400 days, no `secure`).
- **The signed-in redirect is low-risk.** No smoke step requests `/` while signed in; the release check requests `/` anonymously; a signed-in visitor with a flow-state `error_code` already ends on `/dashboard` today (two hops). The middleware strips trailing slashes, so a `"/"` entry in its `AUTH_ROUTES` list would not match as written.
- **Privacy facts are known except vendor regions and plans.** The app stores the account e-mail (Supabase Auth) and group, task and check-off rows; members see each other's e-mails; no analytics; Sentry is server-side with `user.id` only. No user-facing deletion exists, and a manual deletion has side effects: an owner's account cannot be deleted while the group exists, and deleting a member deletes every task they created with other members' check-offs. The Supabase, Sentry and Resend regions and the Workers plan are not recorded in the repo.
- **Legal frame (interpretation, not legal advice).** GDPR applies once the public can sign up (recital 18, C-25/17 ¶42); Art. 13 lists what the policy must state, and several items need owner input. Strictly necessary cookies need no consent or banner under Polish law (Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej art. 399 ust. 3); the 400-day auth cookie is a grey zone. Whether a free, non-commercial service must publish terms ("regulamin") is uncertain.
- **Google Testing access is contradictory and untested.** The Manage App Audience page (re-fetched 2026-10-08) exempts apps that request only name, e-mail and profile, including Sign in with Google, from the test-user list and the warning; other Google pages and the repo's `README.md:265` and `deployment-plan.md:220` say the opposite. Branding edits in Testing are saved as "Draft Branding" and stay invisible to users until verified and published, and verification needs Search Console ownership of every authorized domain, including the redirect URI domain `<ref>.supabase.co`.
- **Tests and docs.** Smoke is the behaviour test (`test-plan.md:99`); it has body and exact-location matchers but no link-target helper, and `location` is a prefix match. The release gate checks `/` by status only. Middleware unit tests exist but run under a Vitest global setup that needs the local Supabase stack.
- **Gaps, closed by the owner's follow-up (see the last section).** Google sign-in stays test-only in S-09; Supabase runs in the EU (Ireland) and every service is on a free plan; the controller is Mariusz Złotucha, contact `mariusz.zlotucha@gmail.com`. Still unknown: the Sentry organization's data storage location (a fallback statement exists) and what a stranger sees after clicking "Continue with Google".

### 2. Contracts around `/` and the signed-in redirect

- **Flow-state redirect.** `index.astro:8-12` sends `error_code` ∈ {`bad_oauth_state`, `bad_oauth_callback`, `flow_state_already_used`} to `/auth/signin?error=oauth_failed` before rendering; any other query renders the page.
- **Middleware order.** The middleware resolves `locals.user` on every request that reaches the Worker (`middleware.ts:16-17`, `:54`) and runs before the page. Its signed-in redirect compares the path after stripping trailing slashes (`:39-41`), so for `/` it compares `""` and `"/"` in `AUTH_ROUTES` (`:9`) would not match. A redirect placed in the middleware runs before the `error_code` check; one placed in `index.astro` can run after it.
- **What a signed-in visitor gets today with a flow-state code.** `index.astro:10-11` → `/auth/signin?error=oauth_failed` → `middleware.ts:39-41` → `/dashboard`. The final destination is already `/dashboard`, so a direct redirect drops no message a signed-in visitor sees today.
- **Auth outage.** With `state.kind === "unavailable"`, `locals.user` is `null` (`middleware.ts:17`) and the failure is reported (`:19-20`); only protected paths answer 503 (`:29-33`). A signed-in visitor on `/` during an outage would see the anonymous page, not the dashboard.
- **Other redirects to `/`.** Only sign-out: `src/pages/api/auth/signout.ts:16` (302 to `/`). The callbacks and the middleware do not redirect to `/` (worker read of `src/`).
- **Smoke (`scripts/smoke.mjs`).** Steps touching `/`: `:346` "home renders" (first step, empty jar, anonymous, status only); `:449-453` three flow-state codes with `cookie: ""` → `locationExact: "/auth/signin?error=oauth_failed"`; `:454-458` unrelated code → 200; `:460` `/` with `cookie: ""` → 200; `:1757` sign-out → `location: "/"`. The jar is signed in from `:541-545` until `:1757`, and no step requests `/` in that window, so a signed-in redirect changes no existing step. `location` is a prefix match (`:1769`), so `location: "/"` matches any path; `locationExact` exists (`:1770`).
- **CI.** The smoke job waits for an anonymous `curl` of `/` (`ci.yml:84`, a loop that does not fail by itself) and runs smoke against the local preview (`:85`). The `release` job's live check (`ci.yml:157-209`) is anonymous `curl`: `/` must answer exactly 200 (`:162-169`), `/dashboard` 302 to `*/auth/signin` (`:170-175`), `/auth/callback` 302 to `*/auth/signin?error=link_expired` (`:176-181`), then a two-hop Google check (`:186-209`). None of these requests reads a body.

- **Tokens and components.** `global.css:13-46` (`:root`) and `:48-80` (`.dark`) define the shadcn palette; no file in `src/` applies the `.dark` class (worker grep), so the app renders light. Font: Outfit Variable (`global.css:9`, `:83-84`). Components in `src/components/ui/`: `alert-dialog`, `alert`, `button`, `card`, `input`, `label`, and an unused `LibBadge.astro`. Auth pages use a `bg-muted` full-screen wrapper with `Card` and `buttonVariants` (`signin.astro:15-31`); the dashboard uses `bg-muted` and Cards (worker: `dashboard.astro:153-155`).
- **In-app wording the copy can match.** "Leaderboard" (`Leaderboard.tsx:29` aria-label), "Total streak points in your group" (`:26`), "You" (`:35`); "Mark done" (`CheckoffControl.tsx:135`), "Done today" / "Done this week" (`:10-11`), "Streak" (`:141`); recurrence "Once", "Daily", "Weekly" (`task-rules.ts:13-15`); "invite link" is the UI name of the join code (`context/domain/glossary.md`, Kod zaproszenia). Capitalisation differs today: "Sign In"/"Sign Up" on `/` versus "Sign in"/"Sign up" on the auth pages.
- **What the product does, for the promise.** PRD vision: several people track the same goal in parallel and see each other's consistency, replacing a shared spreadsheet coloured by hand (`prd.md:18-20`). Business rule: a completed period adds one to the streak, a missed period lowers it without zeroing it, and the sum of streaks ranks members (`prd.md:95-99`); the code halves the streak per missed closed period, rounding down (glossary, Spadek). One group per user (`prd.md:111`, Non-Goals).
- **Promises the code cannot keep.** The glossary lists concepts the code does not know and forbids introducing them without a decision: levels, badges, achievements; notifications and reminders; history, statistics, streak charts, record streak; an individual task as a separate kind; display names or profiles; code rotation, ownership transfer, several groups per user; configurable time zone or decay (`context/domain/glossary.md`, last section). PRD Non-Goals add anti-cheat and offline use (`prd.md:109-114`). The glossary flags "goal, habit, category, todo" as marketing words for what the code calls a task, so they may appear in copy but not in code names.

### 4. Facts a privacy policy has to state

- **Data stored.** Supabase Auth holds the account (e-mail; for Google sign-ins also an `auth.identities` row with name and avatar claims merged into `raw_user_meta_data`, which the app does not read: no use in `src/`, `context/archive/2026-10-05-google-login/research.md:141-144`). Public tables hold user ids, the group name, task titles (free text) and timestamps: `groups.owner_id, name, created_at`; `group_members.user_id, joined_at`; `tasks.created_by, title, created_at`; `task_participants.user_id, joined_at`; `task_checkoffs.user_id, period, checked_at` (`src/types.ts:30-205`). No copy of e-mails or names in the public schema.
- **Who sees what.** `list_group_members` returns `user_id, email, joined_at, is_owner` of current members, and only to members of that group (`20260925161234_add_group_member_list_and_preview.sql:10-31`); the dashboard shows those e-mails in the member list, task participants, the Leaderboard and the remove dialog (worker: `dashboard.astro:144-147,190,206-208,275`, `Leaderboard.tsx:33`).
- **Cookies.** `sb-*` session cookies (library defaults above, 400 days); `join_code` (httpOnly, 1 hour, path `/`, `join-code.ts:4,14-20`); `auth_email` (60 seconds, path `/auth`, `auth-email.ts:3,15-21`); the PKCE code verifier for both e-mail sign-up and Google sign-in (`api/auth/signup.ts:19-20`, `README.md:154`). No analytics or third-party scripts; the font is self-hosted (`global.css:9`).
- **Logs and error tracking.** Workers Logs are enabled (`wrangler.jsonc` `observability.enabled`, no sampling rate) and receive route, user id and `cf-ray` (`src/lib/log.ts:119-129`); e-mails and invite codes are masked (`src/lib/redact.ts:3-11`, `README.md:290`). Sentry runs server-side only (`@sentry/cloudflare` in `package.json`), with `defaultIntegrations: false`, every `dataCollection` flag off and a `beforeSend` that keeps `user.id` only (`src/lib/sentry-options.ts:15-40`). Cloudflare's own invocation logs may keep request URLs with `?code=` or `/join/<code>` (unverified, `context/archive/2026-10-05-google-login/reviews/impl-review.md:139-147`).
- **Processors, regions, retention.** Recorded in the repo: Supabase project ref `wzpgyobsomvjyfrngyuu` (`deployment-plan.md:124,221`), Resend sender `noreply@mail.streakboard.app` (`README.md:256`), Cloudflare registrar, DNS and hosting (`README.md:7`). Not recorded: the Supabase, Sentry and Resend regions and every plan tier (the Sentry DSN is a Worker secret, `README.md:295`). Vendor documentation (worker, fetched 2026-10-08): Workers Logs keep 3 days on Free and 7 on Paid, Free moving to 7 days from 2026-12-01 (developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/); Sentry stores events in the US (Iowa) or the EU (Frankfurt), chosen at org creation, with account metadata possibly in the US, and keeps errors 30 days on Developer, 90 on Team and trials (docs.sentry.io/organization/data-storage-location/, …/data-retention-periods/); Resend stores "all account data, including email metadata, logs, and API records … in the United States regardless of the sending region", 30-day retention on Free (resend.com/docs/dashboard/domains/regions, resend.com/pricing); Supabase contracts as Supabase Pte. Ltd (Singapore) under SCCs Module Two and keeps logs 1 day on Free (supabase.com/legal/dpa, supabase.com/pricing). Transfers outside the EEA therefore happen for at least Resend; Cloudflare, Sentry and Resend name the EU-US Data Privacy Framework with SCCs, Supabase names SCCs.
- **Deletion.** No route, UI or SQL function deletes an account or personal data (worker: `context/changes/data-access/research.md:113,481`). Foreign keys on deletion of an `auth.users` row: `groups.owner_id` **restrict** (`20260925003350_create_groups_and_group_members.sql:15`); `group_members.group_id` and `.user_id` cascade (`:23-24`); `tasks.group_id` and `.created_by` cascade (`20260930120000_create_tasks.sql:20-21`); `task_participants.task_id` and `.user_id` cascade (`20261001090000_create_task_participants.sql:27-28`); `task_checkoffs (task_id, user_id)` cascades from `task_participants` (`20261002090000_create_task_checkoffs.sql:34`). Consequences: deleting a group owner's account fails until the group is deleted, and deleting the group removes its memberships, every task in it and their participation and check-offs; deleting a member's account also deletes every task that member created, with other members' participation and check-offs on those tasks (RLS scenarios, worker: `supabase/checks/rls-scenarios.sql:618-627,1170-1194`). The open domain question Q-09 covers the owner case (`context/domain/01-domain-distillation.md:246`). Leaving a group keeps the account and the e-mail.
- **Contact.** The app publishes no contact address; the one address published anywhere the worker searched (`src/`, `public/`, `README.md`) is the sender `noreply@mail.streakboard.app`. The Google consent screen has support and developer e-mails set, values not recorded (`deployment-plan.md:219`). License: MIT (`README.md:338-340`).

### 5. Legal frame (quoted text and interpretation kept apart)

- **GDPR scope.** Art. 2(2)(c) exempts only "purely personal or household activity"; recital 18: "this Regulation applies to controllers or processors which provide the means for processing personal data for such personal or household activities"; CJEU C-25/17 ¶42: not purely personal "where its purpose is to make the data collected accessible to an unrestricted number of people" (eur-lex.europa.eu, worker). _Interpretation:_ opening sign-up to the public takes the app out of the exemption; the owner is the controller (Art. 4(7)).
- **Art. 13 content** (at the time the data is obtained): (1)(a) controller identity and contact [owner input]; (1)(b) DPO where applicable; (1)(c) purposes and legal basis [owner input]; (1)(d) legitimate interests if Art. 6(1)(f) is used [owner input]; (1)(e) recipients, which here include the other members of the group; (1)(f) transfers outside the EEA and the safeguard; (2)(a) retention or its criteria [owner input]; (2)(b) access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability; (2)(c) withdrawal of consent if consent is a basis; (2)(d) complaint to the supervisory authority (UODO); (2)(e) whether providing data is a contractual requirement; (2)(f) automated decisions. Art. 12(1): "concise, transparent, intelligible and easily accessible form, using clear and plain language". If Art. 6(1)(f) is used, Art. 21(4) requires the objection right "presented clearly and separately".
- **Cookies.** ePrivacy Directive Art. 5(3) exempts storage "strictly necessary in order for the provider of an information society service explicitly requested by the subscriber or user to provide the service"; in Poland Prawo telekomunikacyjne was repealed on 10.11.2024 and Prawo komunikacji elektronicznej art. 399 ust. 3 pkt 2 exempts storage "konieczne do … dostarczania … usługi świadczonej drogą elektroniczną, żądanej przez abonenta lub użytkownika końcowego" from both the information and the consent duty of ust. 1 (Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1221, worker). WP29 opinion WP194 (non-binding, 2012): "Persistent login cookies which store an authentication token across browser sessions are not exempted". _Interpretation:_ `join_code`, `auth_email` and the PKCE verifier are exempt; the 400-day `sb-*` auth cookie is a grey zone.
- **Terms ("regulamin").** Ustawa o świadczeniu usług drogą elektroniczną art. 2 pkt 6 defines "usługodawca" as a person "która prowadząc, chociażby ubocznie, działalność zarobkową lub zawodową świadczy usługi drogą elektroniczną"; art. 8 ust. 1 pkt 1 obliges the usługodawca to set terms (Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1513, worker). _Interpretation, uncertain:_ a private individual running a free, non-commercial service arguably is not a usługodawca; no case law was checked. Google calls the terms link optional on some pages (worker).
- **Minimum age.** GDPR Art. 8(1) sets 16 (the Commission's SWD(2020) 115 lists 16 for Poland, worker) and applies only where consent (Art. 6(1)(a)) is the basis.

### 6. Google: review, Branding, Testing access, button, data policy

- **Safe Browsing review** (developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/security/social-engineering, last updated 2025-12-10, re-fetched 2026-10-08): "A good usability guideline is whether a user viewing the page in isolation understands which site they are on, and the relationship between the first and third party at all times"; "If the report doesn't contain sample URLs and you're confident your site doesn't contain social engineering (deceptive content), request a security review in the Security Issues report"; "A review can take several days to complete". Search Console help (worker, support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9044101): "Fixing the issue on just some pages will not earn you a partial return"; a request "Explains the exact quality issue… Describes the steps you've taken… Documents the outcome"; reviews take "a few days to a few weeks". web.dev (worker, dated 2015): phishing reviews take "about a day", pages "must be available to be crawled by Googlebot". Repeat offenders (worker, …/safe-browsing-repeat-offenders, 2025-12-10): sites that "repeatedly switch between compliant and noncompliant behavior within a short window" cannot request reviews and the status "persists for 30 days".
- **Testing access** (support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945, re-fetched 2026-10-08): Testing apps are limited to up to 100 test users and show a warning to them, but the exception "applies if the app requests only a subset of … name, email address, and user profile": users then do not need to be on the trusted user list, see no warning and their authorizations do not expire after 7 days; "the exception also applies to apps using Sign in with Google". Contradicting pages (worker): …/answer/13464323 ("subject to the unverified app screen and the 100-user cap … when an app is in development/testing/staging") and the brand-verification page ("tester warning screen"). The repo states the strict reading (`README.md:265`, `deployment-plan.md:220`), and the S-07 production checks used only listed accounts (`deployment-plan.md:220`). Not tested live.
- **Branding while in Testing** (worker): edits to "logo, name, home page, privacy policy URI, or authorized domains" are saved as "Draft Branding" (developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/brand-verification, 2026-08-19) and are "not visible to your users until they are verified and published" (support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549049); all domains used "must be pre-registered" as authorized domains, including the redirect URI domains, and an Owner or Editor must verify ownership in Search Console (…/answer/13464321). The redirect URI domain is `wzpgyobsomvjyfrngyuu.supabase.co` (`deployment-plan.md:221`), and `supabase.co` is a public suffix (S-07 research). _Inference:_ entering the home page and privacy URLs is possible but changes nothing users see; brand verification looks unreachable without a Supabase custom domain. Home page and privacy policy links are "required for all external production apps" (…/answer/15549049), so they are the precondition for publishing (In production), which the owner has not decided.
- **Sign in with Google button** (developers.google.com/identity/branding-guidelines, 2026-07-07, worker): text, size and shape comply; fill (≈`#F6F8F8` versus `#FFFFFF`), border (≈`#E3E7E8` versus `#747775`), font (Outfit versus Google Sans/Roboto), the flat four-colour G at 16 px versus the gradient G at 20 px, and the 6 px gap versus 10 px deviate (`GoogleButton.astro:7-26`). The guidelines are "must follow" for verification and "recommended" elsewhere (contradiction between Google pages).
- **User Data Policy** (developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy, February 15, 2024, worker): "You must publish a privacy policy that fully documents how your application interacts with user data" and "thoroughly disclose the manner in which your application accesses, uses, stores, or shares Google user data". ## Architecture Insights

- `/` must stay server-rendered: both the flow-state redirect and a signed-in redirect depend on the request. A privacy page has no request-dependent logic; the adapter serves `dist` as assets (`wrangler.jsonc` `assets`), so prerendering it is possible, but the shared `Layout.astro` reads `missingConfigs` and the Topbar reads `locals.user`, which a prerendered page would evaluate at build time.
- The signed-in redirect has two homes with different orderings: the middleware (central, runs before `index.astro`, needs the `""` path case) or `index.astro` (local, can keep the `error_code` check first). For the requests the current smoke and release checks make to `/` (all anonymous) both give the same result; they differ only for a signed-in visitor with a flow-state `error_code`, where the end state is `/dashboard` either way (directly, or via `/auth/signin`).
- Trust is a property of the whole public surface, not of `/` alone: the review covers the site, the auth pages are where a password is typed, and the template look and assets are shared across `/`, `/auth/confirm-email`, the favicon and `/template.png`.
- The privacy policy is a contract with the data model: its deletion and visibility statements are only true while the foreign keys (`restrict` on owners, `cascade` on creators) and `list_group_members` stay as they are, so a later schema change has to update the page.
